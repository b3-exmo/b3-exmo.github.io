# 🌳 Dallin H. Oaks
## Criticism
> *It’s wrong to criticize leaders of the Church, even if the criticism is true.*

&mdash; *"[The Mormons](https://www.pbs.org/mormons/view/)"*, PBS special ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20200218031209/http://www.pbs.org/mormons/view/)), quote from Dallin H. Oaks; See also [Video Part 2](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/documentary/mormons/#video-2) at 2:08 ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20221012155818/https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/documentary/mormons/#video-2)); See also [*Mormon Newsroom* transcript](https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/elder-oaks-interview-transcript-from-pbs-documentary) ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20130531024624/http://www.mormonnewsroom.org/article/elder-oaks-interview-transcript-from-pbs-documentary)); See also [FAIR](https://fairlatterdaysaints.org/answers/Dallin_H._Oaks/It%E2%80%99s_wrong_to_criticize_leaders_of_the_Church%2C_even_if_the_criticism_is_true) ([archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20210412104419/https://www.fairlatterdaysaints.org/answers/Dallin_H._Oaks/It%E2%80%99s_wrong_to_criticize_leaders_of_the_Church,_even_if_the_criticism_is_true)) insist that it's taken out of context, and then fail to provide context

In the rest of this response to the interviewer Helen Whitney's question, Oaks uses the following *suspiciously specific* illustration:

> *\[D\]on’t depreciate their effectiveness in one area because they have some misbehavior in another area — ==especially from their youth==. I think that’s the spirit of that. I think I’m not talking necessarily just about writing Mormon history; I’m talking about George Washington or any other case. ==If he had an affair with a girl when he was a teenager, I don’t need to read that when I’m trying to read a biography of the Founding Father of our nation==.*

Now... *why* would Oaks choose that as a [[Helen Kimball|valid criticism]] in a hypothetical example? Also, why would we *not* want biographical information in a biography?

&nbsp;

> *Does this counsel to avoid faultfinding and personal criticism apply only to statements that are false? Doesn’t it also apply to statements that are true? In a talk I recently gave to Church Educational System teachers, I urged that “==the fact that something is true is not always a justification for communicating it==.”*

&mdash; *[Criticism](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1987/02/criticism?id=p11&lang=eng#p11)*, Elder Dallin H. Oaks, Quorum of the Twelve, Ensign February 1987

&nbsp;

> *Evil speaking of the Lord’s anointed is in a class by itself. It is one thing to depreciate a person who exercises corporate power or even government power. It is quite another thing to ==criticize or depreciate a person for the performance of an office== to which he or she has been called of God. ==It does not matter that the criticism is true==. As Elder George F. Richards, President of the Council of the Twelve, said in a conference address in April 1947,*
> 
> *“‘==When we say anything bad about the leaders of the Church==, whether true or false, we tend to impair their influence and their usefulness and are thus ==working against the Lord and his cause==.’ (In Conference Report, Apr. 1947, p. 24.)” (Address to Church Educational System teachers, Aug. 16, 1985.)*

&mdash; *[Criticism](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1987/02/criticism?id=p24-p25&lang=eng#p24)*, Elder Dallin H. Oaks, Quorum of the Twelve, Ensign February 1987; see also *["Thou Shalt Not Bear False Witness"](https://scriptures.byu.edu/#:t111:g75)*, Elder George F. Richards, President of the Council of the Twelve Apostles, *Conference Report* April 1947

&nbsp;

Here we have "evil speaking" being *directly connected* to criticizing performance.

We also read from Elder Richards in 1947 that smack-talk only makes leaders of the church perform worse. You're *impairing* their usefulness and are working against Jesus Himself if you ever say anything bad about them.
